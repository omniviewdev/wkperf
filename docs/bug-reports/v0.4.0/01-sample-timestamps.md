# wkperf v0.4.0: profiler samples with `timestamp: 0`

Scope: claim 2 from the previous session ("v0.4.0 keeps only ~357 ms of 2410 ms in `rapid-scroll-deployment.json`"). Every number below comes from commands I ran on v0.4.0 (commit 392facf, installed `wkperf` and a local build from the same commit), v0.3.0 (tag `v0.3.0`, built in a throwaway worktree), or from Python/jq reading the raw exports directly. The big profiles live in `~/Scratch/profiles/` and are not in the repo, so every reproduction below also has a version that uses files in this directory:

- `docs/bug-reports/v0.4.0/fixtures/real-wrapped-fresh-record.json`: real Safari export (byte-identical to `~/Scratch/profiles/fresh-record.json`), 121 of 159 samples untimed.
- `docs/bug-reports/v0.4.0/fixtures/s-untimed-samples.json` (11 KB): synthetic wrapped export, generated by `jq -n -c -f docs/bug-reports/v0.4.0/fixtures/s-untimed-samples.jq`. It has 40 untimed samples and 10 timed samples with known weights (see "Synthetic fixture" at the end).

Terminology: an **untimed sample** is a `recording.samples[].stackTraces[i]` with `timestamp == 0` in a recording whose `startTime > 0`. A **timed sample** has a real timestamp inside `[startTime, endTime)`.

Further facts from the data:

- Every nonzero timestamp lies inside `[startTime, endTime)`. None lies inside a `discontinuities` interval (only `fresh-record` has one: 42.923–236.512). `durations.length == stackTraces.length` in every file.
- The untimed samples are never interleaved with timed ones. In each file they are the first N entries of the array, and they are followed by the timed samples in increasing time order.
- At the boundary, the last untimed sample and the first timed sample share a deep common call prefix: 109 of 110 frames in fresh-record, 8 frames in both pods files, and an identical 5-frame stack in rapid-scroll-deployment (`tick → … → getFiniteAnimations → getAnimations`). Consecutive samples in the same JS task look like this.
- Timed samples are about 1 ms apart while JS runs. In pods-scrolling and rapid-scroll, gap p5/p50/p95 are 1.05/1.31/4.12 ms and 1.07/1.30/2.47 ms, and 91–93% of gaps fall in 0.8–2 ms. That matches JSC's 1 ms sampling interval with ±20% jitter. Timed `durations` are piecewise constant: about half of the equal-duration runs equal exactly `(record.endTime − record.startTime) / samples` for an exported script record.
- The density of untimed samples matches the density of timed samples. I compared samples per millisecond of script-record time before the first timed sample (untimed) with the same ratio after it (timed): pods-scrolling 0.75 vs 0.69, pods-not-moving 0.61 vs 0.57, rapid-scroll 0.69 vs 0.72. In rapid-scroll-deployment, the first timed sample is at +14.908 s of a 17.629 s recording. Script records before it add up to 2972 ms, and 2053 untimed samples × 1.30 ms median spacing ≈ 2.7 s.
- The untimed samples are different samples, not copies of the timed ones. rapid-scroll-deployment has 561 distinct untimed stacks and 67 distinct timed stacks, with 44 in common. `performSyncWorkOnRoot` appears in 1316 untimed samples and 90 timed ones.

### Established from WebKit source (WebKit `main` @ [620b099](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/tree/620b099071f633111ef5b5b294859606ade2ea3a), read, not executed)

- The sample timestamp is the sampler's stopwatch reading at sampling time. [`SamplingProfiler::takeSample`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/620b099071f633111ef5b5b294859606ade2ea3a/Source/JavaScriptCore/runtime/SamplingProfiler.cpp#L371-L375) calls `m_stopwatch->elapsedTimeAndTimestamp()`, and [`buildSamples`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/620b099071f633111ef5b5b294859606ade2ea3a/Source/JavaScriptCore/inspector/agents/InspectorScriptProfilerAgent.cpp#L245-L246) exports `stackTrace.stopwatchTimestamp.seconds()`.
- [`Stopwatch::elapsedTimeAndTimestamp`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/620b099071f633111ef5b5b294859606ade2ea3a/Source/WTF/wtf/Stopwatch.h#L102-L109) returns `m_elapsedTime` when the stopwatch is not running. That value is 0 after `reset()` if the stopwatch has never run. So **`timestamp == 0` means the stopwatch the sampler referenced was not running and had never accumulated time when the sample was taken.** It does not mean "at recording start".
- The page stopwatch is reset and started at `Page.enable`. That is why recording times are small numbers such as 2.6 s or 109 s. WebKit commit [6e52363d99](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/commit/6e52363d99) (2026-10-27) says: "A frame target's stopwatch was never started, so all its stopwatch-based timestamps … were 0." The sampler's stopwatch is replaced by [`setStopWatch` in `startTracking`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/620b099071f633111ef5b5b294859606ade2ea3a/Source/JavaScriptCore/inspector/agents/InspectorScriptProfilerAgent.cpp#L124-L134), and one VM has a single sampler.
- Weights: [`TimelineManager.scriptProfilerTrackingCompleted`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/620b099071f633111ef5b5b294859606ade2ea3a/Source/WebInspectorUI/UserInterface/Controllers/TimelineManager.js#L748-L778) gives `defaultDuration = 1/1000` to every sample whose timestamp is earlier than the first ScriptProfiler event. Samples inside an event get `(event duration) / (samples in event)`. That explains why every untimed sample weighs exactly 1 ms: their 1 ms is a default, not a measurement. Timed weights are per-event averages.
- Order: the sampler appends samples in sampling order (`m_unprocessedStackTraces.append`, [L461](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/620b099071f633111ef5b5b294859606ade2ea3a/Source/JavaScriptCore/runtime/SamplingProfiler.cpp#L461)). [`processUnverifiedStackTraces`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/620b099071f633111ef5b5b294859606ade2ea3a/Source/JavaScriptCore/runtime/SamplingProfiler.cpp#L485-L495) and `buildSamples` keep that order. The frontend [`updateCallingContextTrees`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/620b099071f633111ef5b5b294859606ade2ea3a/Source/WebInspectorUI/UserInterface/Models/TimelineRecording.js#L434-L444) appends each tracking batch with `pushAll` and exports without sorting. **Array order within one target is sampling order.**
- Safari's own call tree filters by the selected time range with `lowerBound(startTime)` ([`CallingContextTreeNode.filteredTimestampsAndDuration`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/620b099071f633111ef5b5b294859606ade2ea3a/Source/WebInspectorUI/UserInterface/Models/CallingContextTreeNode.js#L94-L107)).

### Inferred, not proven

- [INFERENCE] The untimed samples are real JS samples taken during this recording, before the first timed sample. The evidence is the contiguous prefix, the array order guaranteed by WebKit, the matching density and the boundary-stack continuity. The alternative, leftovers from an earlier session, is inconsistent with `releaseStackTraces()` clearing the buffer at every `trackingComplete` and with the density match.
- [INFERENCE] The mechanism is that the sampler used a stopwatch that never ran (for example a frame-target or not-yet-started environment stopwatch) until something switched it to the running page stopwatch. I could not determine which Safari/WebKit build produced these exports (darwin 25.6 WKWebView) or reproduce this in Safari.
- [INFERENCE from source, not run in Safari] Safari's own Call Trees probably hide these samples too, because every selection starts at or after `recording.startTime > 0`. v0.4.0 therefore probably matches Safari's UI. For a root-cause tool that is still the wrong default: it discards 28–85% of the sampled JS weight in four of five real captures with no indication.

## BUG-S1: Whole-recording call trees, rankings, coverage and findings silently drop every zero-timestamp sample

- **Status**: CONFIRMED (claim 2). The claimed numbers are exact: 2053/2282 untimed, 2410.151 ms total, 357.151 ms kept, `getAnimations` 229.1 ms / 134 samples. Part of the claim is wrong: v0.3.0's "~1444 ms top function" was not `getAnimations`. It was the root `(anonymous)` microtask frame, which v0.3.0 reported as *self* time because it read stacks root-first (fixed in Phase 1). The correct v0.3.0 comparison is its total: v0.3.0 kept all 2282 samples / 2410.151 ms.
- **Severity**: blocker. In typical WKWebView captures, 55–85% of sampled weight disappears with no warning. The hot path, top functions and inclusive React cost change in kind, so an agent investigates the wrong code (animation `getAnimations` instead of React sync rendering of `MenuRootInternal2`). An agent verifying a fix gets phantom improvements or regressions from `compare` and `flame --diff` when two captures differ in their untimed share.
- **Version**: v0.4.0. v0.3.0 behaved differently: unwindowed runs kept all samples because `window.at` returned true when no window was active (`git show v0.3.0:internal/analyze/analyze.go`, lines 155-157, sample loop 553-556). Windowed v0.3.0 runs (`--from 0.001`) also dropped them (357.151 ms, 229 samples). The regression came with 9729078 (Phase 1) and 883c2cf (Phase 2). The Phase commits cannot be built one by one: 9729078 imports `internal/flame`, which arrives in 883c2cf; d5c8364 imports `internal/query`; de7b393 imports `internal/rootcause`; 6e9d5c0 has undefined `bindSourceMapFlags`/`loadRecording`. So true bisection is impossible. 883c2cf, the first buildable commit after v0.3.0, already reports `getAnimations` 229.1 ms self on rapid-scroll-deployment.
- **Summary**: `flame.Build` turns each timestamp into a recording-relative time (`0 − startTime < 0`) and keeps only samples inside the active intervals `[0, duration)`, so every untimed sample is discarded. This happens even when no window was requested. Every consumer is built on this tree (analyze, audit, compare, query, MCP, flame, rootcause, insights), so all of them under-count the same way. The derived confidence logic then blames the capture ("Sampling profiler covers little of the script time").

- **Root cause** [VERIFIED by code reading and by the experiments above]:
  - `internal/flame/tree.go:41` computes `timestamp := normalizeTime(trace.Timestamp - origin)`, which is −10 s for the fixture. Then `:93-103` `active := profile.ActiveIntervals(rec, origin+from, origin+to)` (`internal/profile/profile.go:23-59`, at least `[startTime, endTime)` minus discontinuities) and `assemble(selectSamples(samples, active), …)`. `selectSamples` (`tree.go:245-253`, `contains` at `:240-243`) keeps only samples whose relative timestamp is inside an active interval. With no options, `from, to = 0, end` (`:77-91`), so whole-recording builds filter too. This one function feeds `analyze.summarize` (`internal/analyze/analyze.go:482-490`), `insights.Analyze` (`internal/insights/analyze.go:38`), `query.Engine.load` (`internal/query/engine.go:139`), `cmd/flame.go:136-154`, `analyze.RenderFolded` (`internal/analyze/profile.go:162-165`) and `rootcause.Build` (`internal/rootcause/build.go:37-45`).
  - Second-line filters that will also need changing: `rootcause.Build` always calls `tree.Window(from, to)` (`build.go:45`), and `Window`/`InIntervals` use the same `selectSamples` (`tree.go:107-136`). `profile.Clip` (`internal/profile/clip.go:72-86`, `Contains(active, stack.Timestamp)` at `:75`) is called unconditionally by `query metrics` and `query events` (`engine.go:219`, `:231`), even for whole recordings. `cmd/load.go:139-153 clipRecording` (`at(st.Timestamp)` at `:144`) is used by analyze, audit and compare when a window flag is set.
  - Not the cause: `analyze`'s `window.at` (`analyze.go:161-163`, made always-bounded at `:149` in Phase 1) is no longer applied to samples in v0.4.0. It only filters CPU, memory, pressure and markers.
  - Knock-on effects: `SampleCoverage.SampleCount = len(tree.Samples)` (`insights/analyze.go:91`) and `SampledMs` (sum of tree function self, `insights/triggers.go:171-184`) both shrink. As a result, `ruleCoverage` fires (`insights/rules.go:596-605`, "Repeat the same interaction with JavaScript sampling enabled"). `sampledSeverity` downgrades every sampled finding (`insights/evidence.go:39-46`). The confidence reasons "Sparse JavaScript samples" and "Sample weights cover less than half" get added (`evidence.go:189-194`). Comparability warns `sample-sparse` (`internal/compare/comparability.go:112`, `:250-254`).
- **Impact**: wrong in every command that reports sampled data. That covers `analyze` (all formats, hotspots, inclusive hotspots, modules, common stacks, rootCause owners, app attribution, hot path, depth, `low-sample-coverage`, confidence reasons and severities), `audit` (scene and aggregate hotspots: `getAnimations` 298.2 ms where all samples give 963.2 ms), `compare` (funcImproved/funcRegressed and comparability), `query` (top, find, callers, callees, paths, stacks, subtree, hot-path, by-root, depth, diff, metrics `samples.*`, rootcause), MCP (same engine, `internal/mcp/server.go:62-64`), `flame` (html, svg, folded, speedscope, json, `--diff`, `--focus`) and `analyze --format folded`. `summary` and the scorecard do not use profiler samples and are unaffected (checked: no `samples`/function metric keys in `summary --format json`).
- **Proposed fix** (design decision: keep untimed samples in whole-recording aggregates, keep them out of everything time-based, and always report them):
  1. Classification in `flame.Build`: a sample is untimed iff `trace.Timestamp == 0 && rec.StartTime > 0`, or its timestamp is not finite (non-finite samples are currently discarded at `tree.go:42`). Keep the `StartTime > 0` guard: `testgen` and many tests use `StartTime: 0` with a genuine sample at `Timestamp: 0` (for example `internal/report/flame/flame_test.go:142-144`), and those must stay timed. Nonzero timestamps outside the recording or inside discontinuities keep today's behavior (excluded).
  2. Tree representation: add `Tree.Untimed []Sample` (sorted with the existing comparator, so it stays deterministic) next to `Tree.Samples`, which remains timed-only and time-sorted. Add `Tree.UntimedTotal struct{ Count int; Ms float64 }` (always the recording's untimed totals) and `Tree.UntimedIncluded bool`. Do not mix untimed samples into `Samples` with a sentinel time. `rootcause/windows.go:44-46` and `insights/analyze.go:219-223` use `sort.Search` on `Samples`, and periodic, trigger and window detectors read `Timestamp`. A missed consumer would then fabricate temporal evidence instead of under-counting, and fabricated evidence is the worse failure.
  3. Whole vs windowed: `Build` includes untimed samples in `Root`/`byID`/paths/functions iff the selection covers the whole recording, using the tolerance helper from BUG-S3: `From`/`To` absent or `from ≤ 1e-9 && to ≥ duration − 1e-9`, and `Intervals` absent or covering all active intervals. `Window`/`InIntervals` keep `Untimed` only under the same whole-recording test; otherwise they set `Untimed = nil, UntimedIncluded = false`. `FocusNode` and `Inverted` carry `Untimed` through.
  4. Switch aggregate consumers to timed + untimed: `buildNodes` in `assemble`/`Inverted` (`tree.go:149`, `:410`), `aggregate.go:27`, `FocusNode` (`tree.go:170`), speedscope (`internal/report/flame/speedscope.go:57`, `:96-97`; put untimed first, which is their export order), `analyze.go:490` `SampleCount`, `insights/analyze.go:91`, `rootcause/build.go:65-66` (selector), `:78-83` (stats), `ownership.go:21`, `depth.go:37`, `:60`. Keep these timed-only (time-based): `insights/analyze.go:196-216` (sample index used by slow frames and long tasks), `insights/windows.go:154-171` (hot-function context; when the hotspot has only untimed samples, keep the whole-recording context with reason "implicated function has only untimed samples; no time window available"), `rootcause/detectors.go:116`, `periodic.go:114`, `windows.go:44`, `build.go:216-233` stacks `FirstSec`/`LastSec` (an untimed-only stack must not get `exportTime(0)`; emit `firstSec`/`lastSec` as null plus `untimedSamples`), and `insights/triggers.go` via `InIntervals`.
  5. Clipping: `profile.Clip` and `cmd.clipRecording` return `rec` unchanged when the window covers the whole recording (tolerance helper). For partial windows they keep untimed samples in `Samples` but set a new `model.Recording.UntimedExcluded bool` (`json:"-"`). `flame.Build` then counts those samples in `UntimedTotal` and excludes them from aggregates. This keeps counting in one place (`Build`) without a second sample filter.
  6. Reporting (the minimum an agent needs). Insights `SampleCoverage` gets `timedSampleCount`, `untimedSampleCount`, `untimedMs`, `untimedExcluded`.
